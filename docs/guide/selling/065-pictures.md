## They are shrunk in your browser

Before a photograph is uploaded it is resized in the browser, on your machine. A four-megabyte
photo straight off a phone becomes a few hundred kilobytes before a single byte is sent.

Two things follow from that. Uploading is fast even on a bad connection, and your shop stays
fast for customers — a page carrying eight full-resolution phone photographs is a page that
loads slowly on the mobile network most people will open it on.

:::note
You never need to resize anything yourself. Uploading the original is the right thing to do.
:::

## The first one matters most

The first picture is the one used on the shop page, in the basket, in search results and next
to the order. The rest are only seen by somebody already on the product page.

So the first one should be the clearest single view of the thing. Detail shots, scale shots and
in-use shots go after it.

Reorder them with the arrows beside each thumbnail. There is no dragging here, deliberately —
dragging thumbnails on a phone fights with scrolling the page.

## Describing a picture

Each picture has a description field. It is read aloud to anybody using a screen reader, and
shown if the image fails to load.

Write what the picture **shows**: "an ash-glazed bowl on a scrubbed table", not "product
photo". The second one tells somebody who cannot see it nothing at all.

:::tip
It is also what search engines read. A description that says what the thing is does more for
being found than any amount of keywords elsewhere.
:::

## What is accepted

JPEG, PNG, WebP and GIF, up to 8 MB before shrinking.

:::warning
Files are checked by their **contents**, not their name. Renaming something to `.jpg` does not
make it an image, and it will be refused — which is the point: an upload box that trusted the
file name would accept anything anybody chose to call a picture.
:::

## The media library

Everything uploaded is kept in one place, so a picture used on a product can also be used in a
section of the page without uploading it twice. Deleting from the library removes it everywhere
it is used, so it asks first.

:::faq
### What size should my photographs be?

Whatever your camera makes. Square or portrait crops suit product grids best, but the shop
handles any shape.

### Can I use the same picture on several products?

Yes, from the media library.

### Why does my picture look softer than the original?

It was resized for the web. If something genuinely needs full resolution — a print, a detailed
textile — say so and it can be reviewed, but for a shop page the trade is almost always worth it.
:::
