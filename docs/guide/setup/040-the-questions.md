Five questions, once. None of the answers is permanent.

## What do you make

Crochet, cakes, invitations, jewellery, prints, T-shirts, digital downloads, and a few more.
This decides which starting points are offered — a bakery and a jeweller want genuinely
different pages, not the same page in a different colour.

:::note
Pick the nearest one if nothing fits exactly. Every starting point is fully editable, so the
choice narrows what you are shown rather than what you can build.
:::

## What is it called

The shop's name, and the web address that comes from it. The address is checked as you type, so
you find out immediately if it is taken.

`Kiln and Clay Ceramics` → `kiln-and-clay-ceramics` → your shop at that address.

## How you sell today

Instagram, WhatsApp, markets, word of mouth, another platform, or not yet. Nothing is judged
here — it decides which suggestions you get afterwards, like putting your WhatsApp number on
the contact section if that is where people already find you.

## What you want the shop to do

Take payments, take enquiries, or just be somewhere to point people. This is the one that
matters most, because it decides whether you need a checkout, delivery rates and tax rules at
all — and if you do not, you are not asked to fill them in.

:::tip
Not every shop needs a checkout. If everything you make is to order, a catalogue with prices
turned off and a contact button is a smaller thing to maintain and a better fit for how you
actually sell.
:::

## Where you are

Sets the currency and the tax rules. India and rupees by default; the currency is fixed once
orders exist, because an order records what was actually charged and changing the currency
afterwards would rewrite history.

## What happens when you finish

A shop exists — draft, unpublished, and already a complete site with pages, a palette and
lettering. Nothing is on the internet yet.
