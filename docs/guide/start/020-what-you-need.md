None of this is required to begin. You can sign up with nothing but an email address and look
around. But if you want to finish in one sitting rather than three, these are what you will be
asked for.

## Photographs

The one thing you cannot type as you go. Everything else on this list takes a minute to decide;
stopping halfway through to photograph a shelf is what turns twenty minutes into an afternoon.

You need at least one picture per thing you sell, and one wider picture for the top of the
page — a bench, a table, a shelf, your hands working. That wider one does more for a shop than
any other single thing on it.

:::tip
Daylight, near a window, no flash. A phone camera in good light beats a good camera in bad
light, and a plain background — a sheet, a table, a wall — beats a styled one you had to tidy
first.
:::

Pictures are shrunk in your browser before they are uploaded, so you do not need to resize
anything yourself, and a photo straight off a phone is fine.

## What things cost

A number for each thing you sell. If you are not sure, put something down — a price is not a
promise, and changing one later takes two clicks.

If you sell the same item in several sizes or colours at the same price, that is one product
with several pictures rather than several products.

## A few sentences about what you make

Not marketing. What it is, what it is made of, how big it is, how long it lasts. The product
page reads from this, so a description that answers the questions people actually ask you is
worth more than one that sounds impressive.

:::note
If you keep answering the same question in messages — "is it food safe?", "how long does
delivery take?", "can you do it in navy?" — that is the sentence that belongs in the
description. Answering it on the page is cheaper than answering it one person at a time.
:::

## A name, and the address that comes with it

The shop's name, which becomes its web address. `Kiln and Clay Ceramics` becomes
`kiln-and-clay-ceramics`. The address is checked as you type, and you can change it afterwards —
though the sooner you settle it, the fewer people have the old one written down.

## The one decision worth making now

Whether you want people to **buy** on the site, or to **ask** about buying.

A checkout suits anything with a fixed price you can post. Enquiries suit anything made to
order — a cake with a date on it, invitations with somebody's name in them, a commission. The
setup asks you this, and you can change your mind afterwards, but it decides which questions
you get asked next and which parts of the shop you need to fill in at all.

:::faq
### Can I do both?

Yes. Prices can be turned off for some sections and left on for others, so a shop can sell
stock items and take enquiries for commissions on the same site.

### Do I need a bank account to start?

No. Nothing about payments is asked for until you decide to publish, and in this build payments
are simulated anyway.

### What if I only have two things to sell?

That is fine. A shop with two things and good photographs of them reads better than one with
twenty and bad photographs of those.
:::
