BuilderHut is for people who make things — crochet, cakes, invitations, jewellery, prints,
T-shirts, digital downloads — and who want a shop of their own rather than a stall inside
somebody else's marketplace.

You answer a few questions, pick a starting point, change what you want to change, add the
things you sell, and press publish. There is no code at any stage, and nothing you do is
permanent until you publish it.

![The BuilderHut home page, with two miniature shopfronts side by side showing what the builder produces.](shot:marketing-home "Everything on this page is a real storefront, rendered by the same code that renders a live shop.")

## What you end up with

A website at an address of your own, with pages you arranged yourself, a basket, a checkout,
delivery options you set, and an order that arrives in your dashboard the moment somebody buys
something.

:::note
You can also run a shop with no checkout at all. If you take orders by conversation — a cake
with a date on it, an invitation with somebody's name in it — you can turn prices off entirely
and let the site be a catalogue with a contact button. That choice is one of the questions at
the start, and you can change it afterwards.
:::

## How long it takes

About twenty minutes to something you would show people, if you have your photographs ready.

:::steps
### Make an account

An email address and a password. We send you a six-digit code to check the address works,
because an address nobody can reach is an account nobody can recover.

### Answer five questions

What you make, what the shop is called, how you sell today, what you want the shop to do, and
where you are. These pick your starting point and set your currency — they are not a category
you are stuck in.

### Pick a starting point

Twelve of them, one per trade. Each is a complete site — colours, lettering, page structure —
and every part of it is yours to change.

### Add what you sell

A name, a description, a price and a photograph. Pictures are shrunk in your browser before
they are sent, so a four-megabyte photo straight off your phone is not a four-megabyte page.

### Arrange the pages

Sections on the left, the page in the middle, the settings for whatever you clicked on the
right. Nothing is live yet.

### Publish

We check the shop can actually take an order before it goes public, and tell you what is
missing if it cannot.
:::

## What it costs to try

Nothing, and there is no card to enter. You can build an entire shop and look at it before you
decide whether to publish it.

:::tip Before you start
Gather your photographs first. Everything else can be typed as you go, but stopping halfway
through to photograph a shelf is what turns twenty minutes into an afternoon.
:::

## Things worth knowing early

:::faq
### Can I use my own web address?

Yes. Your shop gets a BuilderHut address straight away, and you can point a domain you own at
it afterwards without rebuilding anything.

### What happens if I change something and regret it?

Undo, for the last many steps. And every time you publish, that version is kept — going back is
choosing an earlier one, not undoing your way there.

### Is my shop's look shared with anyone else's?

No. The starting points differ from each other in structure, not just colour, and once you have
changed yours it is yours.

### Do I need to know anything technical?

No. If something in this guide reads as though you do, that is a fault in the guide — tell us.
:::

:::warning
Payments in this build are **simulated**. Orders, their states and refunds are all real and
behave exactly as they will, but no money moves. Connecting a real payment provider replaces
one step and nothing else.
:::
