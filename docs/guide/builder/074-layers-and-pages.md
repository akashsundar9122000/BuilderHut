## Layers

The page as a list, top to bottom. Every section, in order.

Three things it does better than the canvas:

- **Reordering a long page.** Dragging a footer past six sections in the canvas means scrolling
  while dragging. In the list it is one short drag.
- **Hiding.** The eye beside each row. A hidden section keeps everything in it and simply does
  not render — see [adding and arranging sections](/guide/builder/adding-sections).
- **Finding.** Click a row and the canvas jumps to it and selects it.

## Pages

![The Pages tab, showing the shop's pages including the ones that cannot be removed.](shot:builder-pages "Some pages are generated from your catalogue rather than built by hand.")

Your shop has more pages than the ones you made.

**Pages you build.** Home, about, anything else you add. Each is its own stack of sections.

**Pages that are generated.** Product pages come from your catalogue — a new product is a new
page with no layout work at all. The basket, the checkout and the order confirmation are the
same: they exist, they follow your theme, and there is nothing to arrange.

**Pages you cannot delete.** The home page, and the policy pages a shop is required to publish.
They can be edited; they cannot be removed, because a shop without a home page is not a shop and
a checkout without a returns policy is a problem in several countries.

:::note
This is why "product pages are generated, not built" matters more than it sounds. Adding your
fortieth product costs exactly what adding your first did.
:::

## Adding a page

New pages get an address from their name — "Our workshop" becomes `/our-workshop` — and appear
in the header menu unless you say otherwise.

:::tip
Fewer pages is almost always better. A shop where everything is on the home page and the
products are one click away outperforms one with six pages nobody visits. Add a page when you
have something that genuinely does not fit, not before.
:::
