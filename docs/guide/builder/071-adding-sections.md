A page is a stack of **sections**. Each one is a band across the page — a header, a hero, a row
of products, a paragraph, a gallery, a footer. You build a page by stacking them and then
changing what is inside each.

## Where a new one lands

Below whatever is currently selected. That is almost always what you want: you are looking at a
section, you want another one after it.

If nothing is selected it goes at the end.

## The twelve sections

| Section | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Header** | The bar at the top: your name, the menu, a search, an announcement |
| **Hero** | The first thing a visitor sees. A heading, a line, a button, usually a picture |
| **Highlights** | Two to four short points — why these last, how they are made, delivery |
| **Image and text** | A picture beside a paragraph. The workhorse of an about page |
| **Product grid** | Your catalogue on the page, all of it or the newest few |
| **Text** | Your story in your own words. Blank lines make paragraphs |
| **Gallery** | A grid of photographs, with no prices attached |
| **Testimonials** | A quote, a name, and where they are from |
| **FAQ** | The questions you keep getting, answered once |
| **Newsletter** | An email box. Addresses land in your dashboard, not a third-party widget |
| **Contact** | WhatsApp, Instagram and email buttons that open the apps people already use |
| **Footer** | The small print: links, socials, and the policies a shop has to publish |

Header and footer are **structural** — every page has them, and they cannot be removed, only
changed.

## Moving one

Drag it in the canvas, or open **Layers** in the left panel and drag it there. Layers is easier
for a long page, because you can see the whole thing at once instead of scrolling.

Keyboard works too: select a section and use the move controls in the settings panel.

## Duplicating

Useful more often than it sounds. Two image-and-text bands alternating sides is a good-looking
about page, and the second one is a copy of the first with the picture flipped.

## Hiding rather than deleting

:::tip
Hide is the one worth knowing about. A hidden section stays on the page, keeps every word and
picture in it, and simply does not render. It is the right tool for anything seasonal — a
Diwali banner, a "closed until the 8th" notice — because next year it is one click rather than
rebuilding it.
:::

Deleting is permanent for that draft, though undo will bring it back in the same session, and
restoring an earlier version will bring it back later.

:::warning
Deleting a section deletes the words in it. If you are unsure, hide it — the page looks
identical either way.
:::
