Click away from every section — on empty space around the page — and the right-hand panel stops
being about one section and becomes the **theme**: one palette, one type pairing and one set of
shapes, applied to every page of the shop at once.

## Colour, as nine roles

Not a colour picker with nine slots. Nine **jobs**, each of which means something:

| Role | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| Page | The background behind everything |
| Surface | Cards, panels, anything raised off the page |
| Text | Body copy |
| Muted text | Captions, secondary lines |
| Buttons | Every button, and what sits on it |
| Highlights | Links, prices, anything asking to be noticed |
| Borders | Hairlines between things |
| Success | "Added to basket", "paid" |
| Warning | "Only two left", "unpaid" |

Because "Buttons" means something, changing it stays coherent everywhere it lands. A picker with
nine unnamed swatches gives you a shop with four slightly different oranges and no way to find
them all again.

:::tip
Change **Highlights** first. It is the colour a visitor reads as "this shop's colour", and it
does more to make a template feel like yours than the other eight put together.
:::

## Type

Nine faces, paired for you — a display face for headings and a text face for everything else.
The pairing is the identity; most sites use one face for both and look like every other site.

:::note
The fonts are **self-hosted**. A shopper's browser never has to tell a font service who they
are, which is one fewer third party watching people who are only trying to buy a bowl.
:::

## Shape

Three controls, and they do more than they sound like:

- **Corner rounding** on cards and pictures. Sharp reads as gallery; soft reads as friendly.
- **Button rounding**, separately. Pill-shaped buttons on square cards is a real combination
  and a good one.
- **Rhythm** — how much air sits between sections. The single biggest lever on whether a shop
  feels expensive or cramped, and the one people never think to touch.

:::warning
Changing the theme changes every page at once. That is the point, but it means a change that
looks right on the home page is worth checking on a product page before publishing — the
product page has more text on it than anything you designed by hand.
:::

## Your shop's colours are yours

The theme applies to your shop and nothing else. BuilderHut's own dark mode does not reach in
and repaint your brand, and your palette does not leak out into the dashboard.

That is also why a shopper visiting your shop sees your colours regardless of whether their
phone is set to dark mode: a bakery's warm cream is the bakery, not a preference.
