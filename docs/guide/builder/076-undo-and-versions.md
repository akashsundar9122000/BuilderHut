## Saving

There is no save button. The draft saves itself a moment after you stop typing.

The top bar says which state you are in — saving, saved, or a problem. If you close the tab
mid-save, the words you typed are not lost: the save that was in flight completes, and anything
typed after it is kept and sent when you come back.

:::note
Saving is not publishing. The shop the public sees does not change until you publish. See
[previewing and publishing](/guide/builder/publishing).
:::

## Undo

Cmd-Z or Ctrl-Z, or the arrows in the top bar. Many steps back, not one.

**Typing a heading is one step, not forty.** Continuous typing in the same box collapses into a
single undo, which is what you expect and is surprisingly rare in editors. Undoing after a long
sentence takes you to before the sentence, not to the middle of it.

Everything else is one step per action: adding a section, moving one, changing a colour, picking
a picture.

## Version history

![The version history panel, listing published versions with the date each went live.](shot:builder-versions "Each one is an exact snapshot, not a description of what changed.")

Every time you publish, that exact state is kept — not a summary of what changed, the whole
document.

Restoring copies the old version **into your draft**. It does not go live. So you can restore
something from last month, look at it, decide you were wrong, and the public never saw either.

:::tip
This is the safety net that makes experimenting free. Publish, try something drastic, and if it
was a mistake, restore the version from before it and publish again. There is nothing to lose
and no way to get stuck.
:::

:::faq
### How far back does undo go?

Many steps, within the session. Reloading the page starts a fresh undo history — but your work
is saved, so nothing is lost, and version history covers anything older.

### Does undo cross a reload?

No. Undo is for the session; version history is for anything longer.

### Can I name a version?

Versions carry a note, and publishing is the moment to write one. "Before the winter photos" is
worth thirty seconds now and a lot later.

### What if I restore and then change my mind?

Restore a different version. Nothing is destroyed — every published version stays.
:::
