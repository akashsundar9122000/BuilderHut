The top bar has three widths: desktop, tablet and phone. They narrow the canvas.

## They are widths, not emulations

There is no phone simulator here and no separate mobile version of your shop to maintain. The
storefront is responsive — it rearranges itself to whatever width it is given — so narrowing the
canvas shows you exactly what a phone shows, because it is the same page at the same width.

This means two things:

- You cannot make the phone version differ from the desktop version, because there is only one
  version.
- Anything that looks wrong at phone width **is** wrong, right now, for the majority of your
  visitors.

:::warning
Most people will see your shop on a phone. If you only ever look at it on a laptop, you are
checking the version fewer of your customers get.
:::

## What is worth checking

:::steps
### Your heading

A heading that fits on one line at desktop width often takes four on a phone. Shorter is better
anyway.

### The hero picture

A wide landscape photograph becomes a thin strip on a phone. If the subject is off to one side,
it may be cropped out entirely.

### Product grid columns

Four columns at desktop width become one or two on a phone regardless of what you chose, so the
setting is really about desktop.

### Your contact buttons

These are the most-tapped things on a phone shop. Check they are reachable without a long scroll.
:::

## On a phone, the builder itself

![The builder at phone width, with the section panel open as a sheet over the canvas.](shot:builder-panels-phone "The side panels become sheets, because three columns on a phone is three things too narrow to use.")

You can edit from a phone. The two side panels become sheets that slide up over the canvas
rather than columns beside it — three columns on a phone is three things too narrow to be
usable.

Tapping a section on the canvas opens its settings directly, which is faster than it sounds for
small fixes.
