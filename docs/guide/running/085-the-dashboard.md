## The checklist comes first

Until your shop is genuinely ready, the top of the dashboard is a list of what is missing rather
than a chart of nothing.

That ordering is deliberate. A revenue chart showing zero is not information — it is the same
picture whether you have no customers or a broken checkout. The checklist tells you which.

Once the list is done it stops taking up room.

## The numbers

Three tiles and two charts.

| | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Revenue** | Paid, less refunded. Net, not gross |
| **Orders** | Orders placed, whatever state they are in |
| **Conversion** | Visitors who bought, as a percentage |

:::warning
Revenue is **net**. A figure that counts refunded money is a figure that lies to the person
relying on it, and it lies upwards — at exactly the moment you are deciding whether you can
afford something.
:::

## Every comparison says what it compares to

A number with an arrow next to it is meaningless unless you know the period. Every delta here is
labelled — "vs the previous 30 days", not just a green arrow.

:::note
Early on these will swing wildly. Two orders last week and four this week is +100%, and it means
nothing. Percentages start being useful somewhere around thirty orders a month; before that,
read the counts.
:::

## On a phone

The sidebar becomes a bar across the bottom with four things on it: the dashboard, products,
orders and the builder. Four, deliberately — a bottom bar with nine items is a bar where nothing
can be hit with a thumb.

Everything else is still reachable, just not from there.
